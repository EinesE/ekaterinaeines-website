Administrative Continuity Without Identifiable Procedural Closure (Series Note 18)

Ekaterina Eines · First published 20 July 2026

Abstract / From the published PDF

This note examines configurations in which documented administrative developments continue through responses, procedural actions, documentary references, competence relations, or subsequent administrative developments, while the procedural transition necessary for reconstructing procedural closure remains non-identifiable or only partially identifiable.

The observations are descriptive and analytical. They concern identifiability, reconstructability, procedural operativity, substantive reviewability, and documented administrative processes. Particular attention is given to situations in which administrative developments remain observable while the relation between continuing developments and procedural closure cannot be fully reconstructed.

The note does not establish legal conclusions, causal claims, institutional intent, or normative judgments. It may be understood as analytically related to previous work on documentary shielding, mandate gaps, circularity, procedural operativity, substantive reviewability, temporal non-identifiability, structural reproduction, and reconstructability.

Zenodo record description / Copied for this site

This publication is part of the series Public Analytical Notes within the community Criteriology in Practice: Public Analytical Notes, which presents short analytical contributions on evaluation, reviewability, reconstructability, and decision structures within institutional and administrative contexts.

The present note examines situations in which documented administrative developments continue without establishing an identifiable procedural transition through which procedural closure becomes reconstructable. It introduces administrative continuity without identifiable procedural closure as an analytical concept describing configurations in which procedural operativity persists while reconstructable procedural completion remains unavailable.

The note distinguishes administrative continuity from procedural closure and examines its analytical relationship to documentary shielding, mandate gaps, and circularity without treating these as identical phenomena. It further clarifies how analytically distinct configurations may become related through the broader question of reconstructable procedural closure.

The note is descriptive and analytical in character. It does not establish causal claims, normative judgments, or legal conclusions.

The analytical formulation draws on concepts developed within the criteriology framework and Self-Matryoshka Theory (SMT), particularly the methodological vocabulary introduced in Transactions, Transformations, and Transitions (TTT), and may be understood as analytically related to prior work concerning reviewability, reconstructability, procedural operativity, documentary shielding, mandate gaps, and circularity.
